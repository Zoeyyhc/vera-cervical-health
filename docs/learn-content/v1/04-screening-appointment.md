If this is your first cervical screening, or your first one in a long time, you may be feeling some mix of nervous, awkward, or unsure what's about to happen. Most of that can be eased by knowing exactly what the appointment involves. The procedure is short, the discomfort is mild for most people, and there is more flexibility than you might think.

### Before the appointment

**Booking.** You can book through your usual GP, a women's health clinic, a sexual health clinic, or some community health centres. When you book, you can ask:

- Will the appointment be **bulk-billed**? Most are.
- Can a **female provider** do the test? HealthDirect Australia is direct: *"tell them if you would prefer a female to do the test."*
- Should you bring anything specific? Usually no.

**Timing.** Try to book outside your period if you can. It's not always required, but cells are easier to read when there's no menstrual blood in the sample.

**On the day.** Wear something easy to take off below the waist  -  a skirt, dress, or loose pants. The clinic will provide a sheet for coverage.

### Step-by-step at the appointment

The whole appointment usually takes around **10 minutes**. The actual sample collection takes about **30 seconds**.

**1. Arrival and chat.** The doctor or nurse will explain the procedure and ask if you have any questions. This is the time to mention any concerns  -  nervousness, past pain, past trauma. Healthcare staff are used to this and will adapt.

**2. Preparation.** You'll be given privacy to undress from the waist down. The sheet covers your lower body until the test starts.

**3. Position.** HealthDirect describes it directly: *"Lie on your back with knees bent while a staff member explains the procedure."* Some clinics use a position with feet in supports; others just have you bring your knees up. Either is normal.

**4. Speculum.** A small device (metal or plastic) is gently inserted into the vagina to make the cervix visible. This is the part most people associate with discomfort. Tell the clinician if it's uncomfortable  -  they can use a smaller speculum, slow down, or pause.

**5. The swab.** A small soft brush collects cells from the cervix. HealthDirect's guidance: *"This should not hurt. If you do feel any pain, let the doctor or nurse know straight away."* The swab itself takes a few seconds.

**6. Done.** The speculum is removed. You're given privacy to dress. Most people are out the door within ten minutes.

### Self-collection: the other option

Since July 2022, you can choose to collect your own sample using a swab.

**How it works.** You're given a swab and instructions. HealthDirect: *"insert a swab into your vagina and rotate it for 10-30 seconds."*

**What it feels like.** HealthDirect notes: *"The test should not hurt, but it may feel uncomfortable."* Many people find it easier than a clinician collection because they're in control of the timing.

**Where you do it.** Often in a private space at the clinic. Some providers may allow at-home self-collection  -  ask your GP.

**Important:** self-collection is **not** appropriate if you have symptoms (unusual bleeding, unusual discharge, or pain). Symptoms need a clinician examination, not a swab.

### After the test

You may notice **light spotting or mild cramping** for a day or two. This is normal.

Your sample goes to a lab. Results usually arrive within a **few weeks** by SMS or letter, and the result is recorded with the National Cancer Screening Register.

If you haven't heard within a few weeks, contact your GP or call the National Cancer Screening Register on **1800 627 701**.

### Tips if you're nervous

- **You can stop at any time.** Tell the clinician you need a break, or that you want to stop. They will not push you to continue.
- **Bring a support person.** A friend or family member can wait outside or, in many clinics, be in the room.
- **Self-collection takes the speculum out of the picture.** If discomfort with the speculum is your main concern, ask for self-collection.
- **Past trauma is common and healthcare staff know it.** It's okay to mention it briefly. They can adapt the appointment.
- HealthDirect's general advice: *"If you're unsure what to expect, ask the doctor or nurse."*
