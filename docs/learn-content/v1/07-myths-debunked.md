## Goal

Card-style article that knocks down the most common misconceptions one by one. Format: **Myth -> Reality -> 1-line evidence**. Each card should be visually self-contained so readers can scan and share.

## Suggested myths to cover

1. "Only sexually active women need to worry about HPV" -> False (skin-to-skin, not requiring penetrative sex)
2. "If I'm vaccinated, I don't need screening" -> False (vaccine covers most but not all high-risk types)
3. "Cervical cancer runs in my family  -  there's nothing I can do" -> False (HPV is the cause for ~99% of cases; not primarily genetic)
4. "Screening is painful" -> Mostly false (uncomfortable, not painful for most; self-collection now available)
5. "I'm too old / past menopause to need screening" -> False (eligibility runs to age 74)
6. "HPV always means cancer" -> False (most infections clear without treatment)
7. "Only women in same-sex relationships don't need it" -> False (still recommended)

### Card 1: "HPV always means cancer" (most reassuring - lead here)
- **Reality**: 9 out of 10 HPV infections clear on their own within 2 years. Only persistent high-risk infections - over many years - can lead to cancer.
- **Evidence**: 
  - **CDC** direct quote: *"Most HPV infections (9 out of 10) go away by themselves within 2 years."*
  - **WHO** supporting: progression takes "15-20 years" in healthy individuals
- **Source**: CDC + WHO

---

### Card 2: "Screening is painful, I'm putting it off"
- **Reality**: Uncomfortable, yes. Painful for most people, no. The whole sample takes ~10 seconds. And since 2022 in Australia, you can self-collect with a swab if a clinician collection feels too much.
- **Evidence**: 
  - **Cancer Council AU** direct quote: *"Self-collection: You collect your own sample using a simple swab under healthcare provider guidance - equally effective and suitable for those uncomfortable with clinician collection."*
- **Source**: Cancer Council AU
- **Caveat to include**: self-collection isn't appropriate if you have symptoms (bleeding, discharge, pain)

### Card 3: "I'm past menopause, I don't need to keep screening"
- **Reality**: In Australia, eligibility runs to age 74. Cervical cancer can develop at any age, and post-menopausal screening still finds it.
- **Evidence**: 
  - **Cancer Council AU** direct quote: *"You qualify for cervical screening if you are between 25-74 years old, have a cervix, have ever been sexually active. The program applies regardless of...menopausal status."*
- **Source**: Cancer Council AU

### Card 4: "Only people having vaginal sex with men can get HPV"
- **Reality**: HPV spreads through any intimate skin-to-skin contact - vaginal, anal, or oral. People in same-sex relationships, people who've only had non-penetrative contact, and people who've had a single partner can all have HPV.
- **Evidence**: 
  - **CDC** direct quote: *"You can contract it by having vaginal, anal, or oral sex with someone who has the virus, even if they don't have signs or symptoms."*
  - **Cancer Council AU**: *"The program applies regardless of sexual orientation, gender identity..."*
- **Source**: CDC + Cancer Council AU

### Card 5: "I had the HPV vaccine, I don't need to get screened"
- **Reality**: The vaccine protects against the highest-risk HPV types (16 and 18, which cause about 76% of cervical cancers) - but not all high-risk types. Screening catches what the vaccine misses.
- **Evidence**: 
  - **WHO** direct quote: *"All [HPV vaccines] protect against high-risk HPV types 16 and 18, which cause approximately 76% of cervical cancers."*
  - **Cancer Council AU**: program "applies regardless of...vaccination status"
- **Source**: WHO + Cancer Council AU

### Card 6: "Cervical cancer runs in my family - or doesn't - so genetics decide"
- **Reality**: Almost all cervical cancers are caused by long-term HPV infection, not by inherited genes. Family history is much less predictive than your screening status.
- **Evidence**: 
  - **WHO** direct quote: *"Almost all cervical cancer cases result from infection with oncogenic (cancer-causing) types of HPV."*
- **Source**: WHO
- **Note**: Be careful with phrasing - some readers DO have family histories of cancer in general; the point is that cervical cancer specifically is HPV-driven, not genetic.

### Card 7: "I'm in a long-term monogamous relationship, I'm safe"
- **Reality**: HPV can stay dormant for years before being detected. You can carry it from a relationship years ago and only notice now. It's not a sign your partner is cheating.
- **Evidence**: 
  - **WHO**: progression "typically requires 15-20 years"
  - **CDC**: HPV transmission can happen "even if they don't have signs or symptoms" - meaning either partner may unknowingly carry it from past contact
- **Source**: WHO + CDC
- **Why this myth matters**: it's a relationship-friction myth - tackling it head-on prevents harmful misunderstandings

## Content

> **Drafting note:** This is an AI-assisted draft built from the WHO, CDC, and Cancer Council Australia sources cited above. All factual claims map back to direct quotes or paraphrases of those sources. Please review against the source pages before publishing.

A lot of what people "know" about cervical health is out of date or just wrong. Here are seven of the stickiest myths  -  and what the evidence actually shows.

### 01. "If I have HPV, I'm going to get cancer"

**Reality:** Almost all HPV infections clear on their own. Only the small share that persist for many years carry a real cancer risk  -  and screening catches changes long before then.

**Evidence:** *"Most HPV infections (9 out of 10) go away by themselves within 2 years"* (CDC). The WHO adds that progression to cancer *"typically requires 15-20 years"* in healthy individuals  -  a long window in which screening works.

### 02. "The test is painful, I keep avoiding it"

**Reality:** Uncomfortable, sometimes. Painful for most people, no. The whole sample takes around ten seconds. And since 2022 in Australia, you can self-collect with a swab if a clinician collection feels like too much.

**Evidence:** Cancer Council Australia: *"You collect your own sample using a simple swab under healthcare provider guidance  -  equally effective and suitable for those uncomfortable with clinician collection."*

(Self-collection isn't appropriate if you have symptoms  -  bleeding, discharge, or pain. Those need a clinician.)

### 03. "Once you're through menopause, you can stop being screened"

**Reality:** In Australia, eligibility runs to age 74. Cervical cancer can develop at any age, and post-menopausal screening still matters.

**Evidence:** Cancer Council Australia: eligibility *"applies regardless of...menopausal status."*

### 04. "I'm only at risk if I'm having vaginal sex with men"

**Reality:** HPV spreads through any intimate skin-to-skin contact  -  vaginal, anal, or oral. People in same-sex relationships can have HPV. People who've never had penetrative sex can have HPV. People with one lifetime partner can have HPV.

**Evidence:** CDC: *"You can contract it by having vaginal, anal, or oral sex with someone who has the virus, even if they don't have signs or symptoms."* Cancer Council Australia confirms screening *"applies regardless of sexual orientation, gender identity..."*

### 05. "The vaccine covers it  -  I'm done"

**Reality:** The vaccine protects against the highest-risk HPV types  -  16 and 18, which cause about 76% of cervical cancers. It doesn't cover every high-risk type. Screening catches what the vaccine misses.

**Evidence:** WHO: HPV vaccines all protect against types 16 and 18, which *"cause approximately 76% of cervical cancers."* That leaves around a quarter of cases linked to types the vaccine doesn't fully cover.

### 06. "Cervical cancer is in my genes  -  or it isn't"

**Reality:** Almost all cervical cancers are caused by long-term HPV infection, not inherited genes. Family history is much less predictive than your screening status.

**Evidence:** WHO: *"Almost all cervical cancer cases result from infection with oncogenic (cancer-causing) types of HPV."*

### 07. "We're monogamous  -  HPV isn't relevant to me"

**Reality:** HPV can stay dormant for years before being detected. You may be carrying it from a relationship years ago and only see it now. A new HPV diagnosis doesn't mean a partner has cheated.

**Evidence:** WHO notes progression *"typically requires 15-20 years,"* and CDC notes HPV transmits *"even if they don't have signs or symptoms."* Either partner may unknowingly have carried it from past contact.
